5 Ways to Upgrade
Your Pho

AB
Andre Booth // RHMO Writer
May 2024 · 4 min read · Recipes

Pho has been one of my favorite dishes to eat — during a rainy day, after a great event. I can always rely on pho to lift my spirits and have a good time with friends and family.

There's something about a steaming bowl of pho that just hits different. The broth, the noodles, the herbs — it's a dish that feels like a warm hug no matter what's going on. Over the years I've found a few ways to take it from great to absolutely unforgettable.

Here are the 5 ways I use to enhance the flavor and taste of pho:

01

Red Hot Motor Oil

This one goes without saying. A few drops of RHMO into your pho brings a smoky, nutty heat that works with the broth instead of fighting it. Start with a drizzle and build from there — it layers beautifully with the existing spices and aromatics already in the bowl. This is the single biggest upgrade you can make.

// The Move

Add RHMO right after the bowl lands in front of you. Stir it in slowly so the oil blooms through the broth. You'll see it and smell it immediately.

Get a Bottle →
02

Fresh Jalapeños

Thin-sliced fresh jalapeños add a bright, clean heat that's completely different from dried chili. Drop them in right away so they soften slightly in the broth, or keep them on the side and add as you go. Either way, they bring a freshness that lifts the whole bowl.

03

Extra Hoisin on the Side

Most spots give you a small side of hoisin but don't tell you how to use it right. Don't pour it into the broth — dip your meat in it. The sweetness and depth of hoisin paired with a bite of beef and RHMO is one of those combinations that makes you stop mid-conversation.

04

Bean Sprouts — Added Late

Most people dump the bean sprouts in immediately and they get soft and mushy. Add them about halfway through the bowl so they stay crunchy. That texture contrast against the soft noodles and tender meat is what separates a good bowl from a great one.

05

A Squeeze of Lime at the End

Save your lime wedge for the last third of the bowl. By that point the broth has cooled slightly and the acid from the lime cuts through the richness in a way that makes everything taste cleaner and brighter. It resets your palate and makes the last few bites hit just as hard as the first.

That's it. Five moves, all easy, all worth it. Whether you're at your favorite spot or making it at home, these are the small details that turn a good bowl of pho into a great one. And none of them work quite as well without Red Hot Motor Oil on the table.

Try it and let us know what you think.

— Andre Booth

Taco Tuesday
Done Right

AB
Andre Booth // RHMO Writer
April 2024 · 4 min read · Food

Taco Tuesday — one of the best days of the week. The week just started and you have three days to go until the weekend. This dinner is just what I need. When I get my tacos the first thing I'm reaching for is RHMO. A little truly goes a long way by adding this to boost the flavor of any taco. Al Pastor, Birria, Chicken — just to name a few. I always look forward to adding RHMO to my tacos.

There's something about Taco Tuesday that just resets the week. Doesn't matter how rough Monday was. Once those tacos hit the table, everything else can wait. And for me, the moment that actually makes the night is reaching for the bottle of RHMO and getting to work.

I've tried a lot of hot sauces, salsas, and condiments on tacos over the years. Nothing has stuck the way RHMO has. The reason is simple — it doesn't compete with the taco. It elevates it. The chili oil works with whatever protein, salsa, or topping you already have going on and adds a layer of smokiness and heat that makes everything taste more intentional.

Why RHMO Is the Perfect Taco Companion

01

Al Pastor

Al Pastor is already one of the most flavorful tacos on the menu — marinated pork, pineapple, achiote, slow-cooked on a trompo. It's sweet, savory, and slightly tangy all at once. RHMO takes that and adds a smoky heat that cuts through the sweetness without drowning out the pineapple. One drizzle and you'll wonder why you ever ate al pastor without it. This is the combination I come back to the most.

02

Birria

Birria is rich, deep, and built on hours of braising. The consommé alone is a whole experience. RHMO on a birria taco — especially one that's been dipped and griddled — adds a layer of nutty heat that plays off the rendered beef fat in the best way. Dip the taco in the consommé, drizzle RHMO over the top, and take a bite. That's it. That's the whole argument.

// The Move

Add RHMO after the dip, not before. You want the consommé flavor to come through first, then the heat from the chili oil hits at the end. The layering is what makes it. Don't skip this step.

Get a Bottle →
03

Chicken

Chicken tacos tend to be lighter and cleaner than pork or beef — which means they're actually the perfect canvas for RHMO. The chili oil's nuttiness and smokiness come through more clearly on chicken because there's less fat competing with it. Whether it's grilled, adobo-marinated, or crispy fried chicken in a taco, RHMO adds exactly the kind of bold character that makes it memorable. This is also the combination I recommend to people who are trying RHMO for the first time.

04

A Little Goes a Long Way

This is worth saying out loud because it's easy to overdo it the first time. RHMO is concentrated flavor — you don't need to drown the taco. Start with 3 or 4 drops along the length of the taco. That's your baseline. From there, you'll know if you want more heat. The oil distributes with each bite, so a small amount carries through the whole taco. Respect the bottle and it'll respect your food.

05

Works on Every Taco You Can Think Of

Carne asada. Carnitas. Shrimp. Fish. Barbacoa. Lengua. Vegetarian with roasted peppers and black beans. It doesn't matter. If it's in a tortilla, RHMO belongs on it. The oil is versatile enough to work across every protein and preparation style because it's adding depth, not just heat. That's what makes it different from a standard hot sauce — it's a flavor enhancer first, and the heat is part of the experience, not the whole thing.

Taco Tuesday is already a great tradition. RHMO just makes it better every single week. Whether you're hitting your favorite spot, cooking at home, or doing a full spread for friends — grab the bottle first. The tacos will thank you.

Try it and let us know what taco you put it on first. We want to know.

— Andre Booth

Chili Oil
Mac n' Cheese

AB
Andre Booth // RHMO Writer
March 2024 · 5 min read · Recipes

I really enjoy mac n' cheese — and by putting RHMO on it, it enhances the flavor so much. It's a blend between sweet, savory, and spicy. People who are a fan of all three will definitely enjoy this.

Mac n' cheese is one of those dishes that hits different depending on your mood. Some days it's comfort food. Some days it's a late-night situation. But no matter when I'm eating it, the one thing that has never changed is that RHMO makes it better. Every single time.

The reason it works so well comes down to contrast. Mac n' cheese is rich, creamy, and naturally sweet from the cheese. RHMO brings smokiness, nuttiness, and a slow-building heat that cuts right through that richness. The result is a bowl that hits multiple parts of your palate at once — and once you've had it that way, plain mac n' cheese starts to feel like something's missing.

Why RHMO and Mac n' Cheese Work So Well Together

01

The Sweet-Savory-Spicy Trifecta

Most people think of mac n' cheese as a one-note dish — cheesy, buttery, heavy. And it is all of those things. But that's exactly why RHMO belongs on it. The chili oil brings two things mac n' cheese doesn't have on its own: heat and depth. The sweetness of the cheese, the savoriness of the butter and pasta, and the spicy nuttiness of RHMO create a flavor combination that feels intentional. Like it was designed that way.

02

How to Apply It

Don't just pour it on top and call it a day. Drizzle RHMO across the surface right before you eat — not while it's still cooking. You want the oil to sit on top of the cheese sauce so every bite has the right ratio of heat to creaminess. Start with a light drizzle, mix one spoonful to test the heat level, then add more if you want it bolder. The oil distributes through the bowl as you eat, so it gets better as you go.

// The Move

Drizzle RHMO in a zig-zag across the top right before serving. Don't stir it in all at once — let each bite have its own balance of cheesy and spicy. The uneven distribution is actually the point.

Get a Bottle →
03

It Works on Both Homemade and Box Mac

This isn't a "fancy ingredients only" situation. Whether you're making it from scratch with a roux and sharp cheddar, or you're working with a box of Kraft at midnight — RHMO works on both. The oil doesn't care how the cheese got there. It just makes it better. That's the thing about a great condiment: it meets the dish where it is and elevates it from there.

04

Try Adding a Little Crunch

If you want to take it one step further — toasted breadcrumbs on top with RHMO drizzled over everything right before serving is genuinely next level. The crunch adds texture, the oil adds heat, and the cheese sauce underneath ties it all together. It's a simple addition but it turns a bowl of mac n' cheese into something you'd actually put on a menu.

05

Who This Is For

If you love sweet, savory, and spicy — this combination was made for you. If you're someone who adds hot sauce to everything but hasn't tried a chili oil yet, RHMO is the upgrade you didn't know you needed. It's not just heat — it's flavor. And on mac n' cheese, it's the kind of flavor that makes you stop and say "wait, what did I just put on this?"

Mac n' cheese has always been a comfort food. RHMO keeps it that way while adding something that makes it feel new every time. The sweetness of the cheese, the savoriness of the pasta, and the spicy depth of the chili oil — it's a combination that just makes sense.

Try it once and you'll never go back to plain mac n' cheese again. And when you do, tag us. We want to see it.

— Andre Booth
